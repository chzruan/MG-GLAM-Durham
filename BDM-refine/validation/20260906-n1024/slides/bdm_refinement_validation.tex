\begin{frame}{Appendix: a concrete duplicate-survival case}
In the earlier $128^3$ integration test, three close candidate pairs survived
exact-membership removal because their particle sets differed slightly.
\medskip\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}\toprule
Pair & Shared / smaller set & Separation $[\mpch]$ & Host radius $[\mpch]$\\\midrule
1 & $2109/2110$ & $0.0035$ & $1.216$\\
2 & $326/329$ & $0.0245$ & $0.699$\\
3 & $1441/1445$ & $0.0155$ & $1.082$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\medskip
\resultbox{Equal-mass host priority}{Rank hosts by stored bound mass; resolve an exact mass
tie with a stable candidate order. Suppress the lower-priority centre strictly
inside the higher-priority host radius. All three extra entries disappear.}
{\small This small-box result explains the repair; it is separate from the new $1024^3$ validation.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Appendix: numerical sensitivity is isolated from finder threading}
\begin{itemize}
\item The $512^3$ pilot's ordinary 16/32-thread replays differ in $2/71{,}587$ rows,
with identical bound masses, counts and bulk velocities.
\item With a fixed density field, the tested 16/32-thread finder calls are
byte-identical; changing only the field reproduces both rows.
\item The initial centring radii of those two rows differ by one float32 step.
This small input change can affect later centring and internal properties.
\item \MainNumericalAppendix
\end{itemize}\medskip
\resultbox{Shape ordering}{Different empirical corrections could give $c/a>b/a$.
Sorting the two corrected lengths restores their labels while preserving both
values, the major direction, every other property and membership.}
\end{frame}
